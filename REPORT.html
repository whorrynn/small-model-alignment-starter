14.1Repository, Models, and Data

  • Base checkpoint: Qwen/Qwen2.5-0.5B (base, not instruct). LoRA base: HuggingFaceTB/SmolLM2-360M-Instruct.
  • SFT data: HuggingFaceH4/ultrachat_200k, split train_sft (207,865 rows), shuffled with seed 42, first user/assistant exchange only. 30,000 train / 1,000 val.
  • HH-RLHF data: Anthropic/hh-rlhf train (160,800 rows), shuffled seed 42, single-turn rows only (parsed from the shared conversation string). 15,000 train / 1,000 val pairs.
  • UltraFeedback data: HuggingFaceH4/ultrafeedback_binarized train_prefs, shuffled seed 42, single-turn rows only. 15,000 train / 1,000 val pairs.
  • Persona data: data/persona_lora/ramsay_style_preferences.json (220 rows): first 90% (198) train, last 22 eval.
  • Fixed eval sets: eval_sets/hh_rlhf_eval_300_seed42.json (provided; 10 categories × 30) and eval_sets/alpaca_eval_300_seed42.json, built once by src/alpaca_eval_subset.py (805-row alpaca_eval.json → shuffle seed 42 → first 300), reused by every later stage.
  • Seeds: 42 everywhere — data shuffles, torch/numpy/random, pairwise A/B coin flips.
  • Reproducibility choices: one prompt template (below) used for SFT, DPO, and all Qwen generation; right-truncation of prompt+response at 512 tokens (a prompt that fills 512 tokens alone yields zero response tokens and zero loss); EOS appended to every response; tatsu-lab/alpaca_eval (script-based, rejected by datasets>=4) is downloaded directly as alpaca_eval.json instead, giving identical rows.

14.4Training Behavior

  • Shape of the curve: val loss starts at 344.80 (base model), rises to 353.08 by step 150 during warmup, then falls through cosine decay to 339.16 at step 900 (339.15 final, step 938).
  • Early rise (hypothesis): most likely the model adapting to the new response format — learning to end with EOS after the Response: header before it improves on content, temporarily giving back some of the base model's raw continuation ability.
  • No overfitting: train/val never diverge in a way suggesting overfitting; this is one epoch over 30k examples, so each example is seen once.
  • Best checkpoint: lowest val loss among evals every 50 steps → step 900 (best vs. final differ by 0.01, i.e. noise).
  • Scale of improvement: only 1.6% net gain in summed NLL. The loss is a sum over response tokens, so absolute values (~340 nats) mostly reflect UltraChat response length; a 0.5B base model already models this text well, and SFT mainly changes format/stopping behavior, which a per-token-mean loss would understate.
  • Reproducibility: an independent first run (v6, same seed/config, eval batch 8) reached 339.27 at step 900 vs. 339.16 for the submitted run (v8).

  • Step-0 sanity check: policy = reference, so loss is exactly log 2 = 0.6931. Reward accuracy is exactly 0 (not 0.5) because every pair ties and accuracy uses strict >, per manual §10.5 — the fp32 reference is what makes these ties exact.
  • Learning speed: both runs learn fastest in the first 50 steps (~1,600 pairs); accuracy then rises slowly while margin keeps growing. At β=0.1, lr=1e−6, the policy moves only slightly from SFT in one epoch, with smooth validation curves and no spikes or reversals beyond eval noise.
  • UltraFeedback vs. HH-RLHF: UltraFeedback learns ~2.4× the margin (0.475 vs. 0.198) and reaches higher accuracy (0.664 vs. 0.618) — consistent with GPT-4-rated completions having a clearer quality gap than often near-duplicate, noisier single-turn HH-RLHF pairs.
  • Overfitting signal: UltraFeedback val loss bottoms at step 350 (0.5840) and ticks up slightly by step 450 (0.5898) while accuracy still rises — a mild early sign of over-optimization, not real overfitting. HH-RLHF improves monotonically on all three metrics. Checkpoint selection (highest val reward accuracy) picks step 450 for both.

Integrity and robustness checks

  • Pair files: every response_a/response_b matches the SFT/DPO generation its hidden position_to_model says it does (0 mismatches).
  • Coverage: every pair judged exactly once; recount from raw verdicts matches results/summary.json.
  • Identical outputs: all 22 pairs where SFT and DPO text matched exactly were judged ties.
  • Significance: HH-RLHF significant (sign test on non-ties, p=0.0002); UltraFeedback deficit is not (p=0.096).
  • Duplicate prompts: the HH eval file has only 185 unique prompts (71 appear 2–3×); greedy decoding makes duplicated outputs identical. Per-unique-prompt HH win rate: 0.579 ± 0.056 — conclusion unchanged.
  • Position bias: among non-tie verdicts the judge picked slot A 57% (HH) / 64% (AlpacaEval) of the time; 15/71 duplicated HH prompts got different verdicts under a different A/B order. DPO is balanced across slots, so this adds variance but should not shift the mean much.

LoRA persona (separate target: style, not helpfulness)

The submitted adapter (v10) reached held-out reward accuracy 1.00 and margin 1.74 on the 22 persona pairs. Qualitatively, on four fixed style prompts (results/cs5326-pa2-lora/project/outputs/lora_persona/style_eval_samples.json) the persona shows up as a more direct, conversational voice — e.g. “Ah, you're feeling down, huh? Well, you're not alone, mate.” vs. the base model's “Of course, I'd be happy to help you with that.” It stays helpful on cooking, coding, and study-planning, and supportive on the self-harm-adjacent prompt. It does not reach the insult-heavy style of the chosen training responses — the stronger configuration (v9) that did adopt that voice collapsed into repetition (§14.3).

14.6Interpretation and Error Analysis

Measured: HH-RLHF DPO beats SFT on the safety-oriented eval (0.59 overall), concentrated in medical_safety (0.87), cyber_abuse (0.70), and benign_helpfulness (0.65); flat or slightly negative on harmful_instructions (0.48), calibration_and_uncertainty (0.47), legal_financial and safe_transformation (0.50). UltraFeedback DPO does slightly worse than SFT on AlpacaEval (0.46, not significant), despite better training-time diagnostics (accuracy 0.664 vs. 0.618, margin 0.475 vs. 0.198).

Interpretation (hypotheses, not proven): HH-RLHF DPO mostly taught the model to stop and be gentler in tone rather than to refuse — responses got shorter and hit the token cap far less, so fewer were cut off mid-sentence or looping, which alone favors DPO under a judge that rewards concision and coherence. Explicit refusals barely moved (harmful_instructions stayed at 0% refusal phrases and is the weakest category); at β=0.1, lr=1e−6, one epoch, noisy single-turn HH pairs (val accuracy only 0.62), a 0.5B model learns easy surface preferences (shorter, gentler) far more than hard ones (decline and redirect). UltraFeedback DPO appears to have learned a length preference — its chosen responses are typically longer/more elaborate, a known DPO shortcut. Our outputs got 20% longer and half now hit the 256-token cap; a truncated or over-elaborated answer from a 0.5B model is often worse than SFT's shorter one, an early case of reward over-optimization consistent with UltraFeedback's val loss bottoming out at step 350.

14.7Limitations and Reproducibility

Limitations

  • Single judge, single seed, small/partly duplicated eval: one LLM judge with measurable position bias (57–64% slot-A preference; 15/71 duplicated HH prompts flipped verdict under reordering); one training seed per run (only repeat is SFT: 339.27 vs. 339.16 val loss across two independent runs); 300 prompts/track, HH file has only 185 unique; the ±0.05 CIs cover sampling noise only, not judge or seed variance.
  • Greedy decoding, no repetition controls for any model, kept identical across SFT/DPO for fairness — leaves 26–33% of outputs with degenerate repetition and many truncated at 256 tokens. This affects SFT and DPO alike but lets the judge partly reward “stops cleanly” over content. A repetition-penalized decode (supported by scripts/generate_eval_outputs.py) was not run.
  • Short DPO training: one epoch at the guide's lr=1e−6, β=0.1, with no tuning for the full-FT tracks; the HH result likely understates DPO's ceiling for refusals. max_prompt_length was not applied (§14.3).
  • Refusal measurement is a regex over phrases — a rough proxy; the judge remains the primary measure.
  • Compute / failed runs (all Kaggle T4×2): SFT needed three runs (final-eval OOM, periodic-eval OOM, clean run); LoRA needed several fixes (DataParallel, bf16, prompt template) plus a three-point sweep; Kaggle only exposes outputs of a kernel's successful versions, forcing the SFT rerun.
